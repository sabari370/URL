Defensive Cyber Hygiene

Essential Phishing Prevention Guidelines

Technical machine learning tools assist in detection, but active vigilance and security awareness remain your strongest perimeter defense against social engineering.

1. Scrutinize the Registered Domain

Attackers often prepend familiar brands as subdomains (e.g. paypal.com.verify-user.xyz). Always read the domain name from right to left to locate the true root authority.

2. Never Equate HTTPS with Safety

HTTPS only encrypts communication between your browser and the server. It does not prove the server owner is honest. Over 80% of modern phishing websites utilize valid TLS certificates.

3. Beware of Manufactured Urgency

Phishing attacks rely on artificial panic: "Account suspended in 24 hours," "Unauthorized transaction detected," or "Password expiring today." Step back and verify before reacting.

4. Navigate Manually to Critical Services

For banking, cloud storage, email, or government portals, never click links inside emails or SMS messages. Open your browser and type the trusted domain address yourself.

5. Exercise Caution with QR Barcodes

Physical stickers over legitimate parking meters, restaurant menus, or unsolicited letters can redirect your device to credential harvesters. Use PhishGuard AI's QR tool to preview links first.

6. Expand Shortened URLs

URL shortening platforms (bit.ly, tinyurl) mask the true destination. If you receive an unexpected short link, use link-expansion tools or avoid opening it altogether.

7. Mandate Multi-Factor Authentication

Enable hardware security keys (FIDO2/WebAuthn) or authenticator apps (TOTP). MFA prevents attackers from gaining unauthorized access even if your password is stolen.

8. Spot Homograph / Typosquatting Tricks

Look for character substitutions: rn imitating m, 0 replacing o, or Cyrillic letters that appear identical in Latin alphabets (IDN homograph attacks).

9. Block Unexpected Download Prompts

Legitimate websites rarely prompt an instant file download upon landing. Never execute downloaded .iso, .exe, .vbs, or macro-enabled documents from unknown links.

10. Hover to Preview Link Targets

In desktop email clients and web browsers, hover your cursor over hyperlinked text before clicking. Verify that the URL shown in the status bar matches the link's anchor text.

11. Keep Browsers & Systems Patched

Modern browsers include built-in reputation filters and zero-day rendering patches. Ensure automatic updates are enabled for your browser and underlying operating system.

12. Report Phishing Promptly

If you identify a phishing attempt at work or school, report it to your IT security team or forward it to global reporting feeds like Google Safe Browsing and the Anti-Phishing Working Group (APWG).
